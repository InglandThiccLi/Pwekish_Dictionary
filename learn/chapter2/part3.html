Part 3: Irregular Letter Groups

The Rest Are Irregular

Now that we have covered the regular groups, it is time to look at the irregular letter groups. Letters in these groups do not follow the strict "voiceless -> voiced -> nasal" sound pattern. Because the acoustic relationships are less predictable, they require a bit more effort to memorize.

Except for "p, b, m", "t, d, n", and "k, g, ņ", all other letter groups in Pwekish are irregular. Here is the complete list:

“ f, v, w ”
“ s, z, r ”
“ h, x, ' ”
“ l, y, ñ ”

The exact same shifting procedure applies when conjugating these verbs. Let's take "xepte" /ˈʁepte/ (to swim) as an example to find its 2nd person mutation:

  1. Identify the Group: It starts with "x", so it belongs to the “ h, x, ' ” group (h -> x -> ').
  2. Determine the Shift: For the 2nd person mutation, the shift amount is 1.
  3. Rotate the Sound: Shifting "x" by 1 moves it to the “ ' ” slot.
  4. Apply the Marker: Even though the “ ' ” slot historically functions like the "nasal" slot in the structural paradigm, the target sound is a glottal stop /ʔ/. Because of its position, we apply the nasal marker "n-" to the root letter, giving us "nxepte", which is pronounced /ˈʔepte/ .

The "C" Letter Group

Words starting with "c" belong to a unique irregular group: "c, z, ņy". The pronunciations for these slots are /s/, /z/, and /ŋʲ/ respectively.

For example, the PVIM mutations for "cirlo" /ˈsiɬo/ (to occur) are:

  • 1st Person: cirlo /ˈsiɬo/
  • 2nd Person: hcirlo /ˈziɬo/
  • 3rd Person: ncirlo /ˈŋʲiɬo/

Crucial Note: The "C" letter group applies only to words starting with "c" and "ņy". It does not apply to words starting with "z" (which always belong to the "s, z, r" group).

You might also notice that "ņy" is a compound letter. Pwekish does have specific rules for compound letters, which we will discuss in the next section.

The Vowel Letter Groups

Be extra careful when dealing with words that start with a vowel. Vowels belong to a special structural letter group designated as "Ø, h, x" (where Ø represents any vowel). Unlike other groups, the marker for the third-person mutation in the vowel group is "hh-" instead of "n-".

For example, the PVIM mutations for "anu" /ˈanu/ (to know) are:

  • 1st Person: anu /ˈanu/
  • 2nd Person: hanu /ˈhanu/
  • 3rd Person: hhanu /ˈʁanu/

Don't Mix Them Up! Do not confuse the vowel group “ Ø, h, x ” with the consonant group “ h, x, ' ”. The “ Ø, h, x ” group is exclusively for words that start with an actual vowel. If a word starts with the physical letters "h" or "x", it strictly belongs to the “ h, x, ' ” consonant group.

Reminder: Do you still remember how accent marks work? (For example, the ú in vurún). They indicate word stress. If no accent mark is present, the stress automatically falls on the second-to-last syllable by default. Also, keep an eye out for the letter "ə" (which produces the /ə/ sound). Even though it rarely appears, it follows these same structural rules!
