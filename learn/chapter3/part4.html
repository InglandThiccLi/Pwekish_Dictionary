Part 4: Suffixes

Suffixes

Pwekish is an agglutinative language. It relies extensively on a wide range of affixes to construct new words and support different grammatical functions. We have already learned about several affixes in previous sections, including PVIM markers, tense prefixes, the adjectivizer, and the nominalizer. In this part, we will focus on a few useful suffixes.

Negative Marker

Up until now, we haven't covered how to "say no" in Pwekish. This is because negation is achieved structurally by adding the suffix "-ne" /ne/ .

Before learning how to use this suffix, let's look at how to say standalone "Yes" and "No":

✅ pé /ˈpe/ yes
❌ né /ˈne/ no

"pé" is used to agree with a statement, while "né" is used to disagree. When writing these words, the accent mark is strictly required. Without it, the words have completely different meanings.

The suffix "-ne" negates the meaning of the word it attaches to. It can be applied to verbs, nouns, adjectives, and adverbs of manner, functioning much like the English words "don't," "no," "not," or the prefix "un-".

There is one major exception when negating the verb pe (to be). The correct negated spelling is "pne", not "pene".

Example Sentences

Sentence Meaning
Ņra yenmor sile sanone. I don't have a big car.
Ņra yenmorne sile sano. I have no big car.
Yenmor ņrae nsilne. My car is not big.
Ņra yenmor silne sano. I have an unbig (small) car.
Yenmor hmor tyerne. The car goes unquickly (slowly).

When "-ne" is applied to an adjective root or an adverb of manner, we do not add the adjectivizer "-e". For instance, the root sil (big) becomes silne (not silene), and the root tyer becomes tyerne (not tyerene).

Unlike the adjectivizer "-e", the suffix "-ne" alters the stress pattern of a word. Unless the word has an accent marker, "-ne" always makes the resulting word to have a stress on the second-last syllable.

Plural Marker

In English, we typically add "-s" or "-es" to a noun to make it plural. In Pwekish, marking plurality is not mandatory. However, the language uses a specific plural marker when a speaker wants to emphasize that there is more than one thing, especially if the amount is quite large.

To make a word plural, add the suffix "-res" /res/ . Fascinatingly, this plural marker is highly versatile and can be applied to nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs of manner.

Example Sentences

Sentence Meaning
Ņra bimbores dip. I eat (a lot of) apples.
Ņra bimbo dipres. I eat apples a lot.
Ņra bimbo silres dip. I eat a very big (huge) apple.
Ņra bimbo dip tyerres. I eat apples very quickly (rapidly).

The first example emphasizes the number of apples while the second example emphasizes the frequency of eating apples. When the plural marker is used on an adjective like the third example, it acts like the word "very".

Just like the negative marker, when "-res" is applied to an adjective root or an adverb of manner, we no longer add the adjectivizer "-e". And "-res" also alters the stress pattern like "-ne" does.

Intensifier

We just learned that adding the plural marker "-res" to an adjective intensifies its meaning into "very". Pwekish also features a dedicated, distinct intensifier suffix: "-os" /os/ . This suffix can only be attached to verbs, adjectives, and adverbs of manner.

While both can amplify a word's meaning, they carry different emotional weights:

  • "-res" is objective and neutral.
  • "-os" is subjective, emotional, and expressive.

Because of this emotional layer, "-os" is generally interpreted as a stronger, more intense version of "-res".

Example Sentences

Sentence Meaning
Ņra bimbo nukeos. I really like apples.
Bimbo nsilos. The apple is so big.
Yenmor hmor tyeros. The car goes so quickly.

Like "-ne" and "-res", you do not add the adjectivizer "-e" to adjective roots or adverbs of manner when using "-os". It alters the stress pattern as well.

Reduplication (Alternative Intensification)

For shorter words, you can further intensify a meaning by completely repeating (reduplicating) the word instead of using the suffix "-os"。

Example Sentences

Sentence Meaning
Ņra bimbo nuke nuke. I really really like apples.
Bimbo nsil nsil. The apple is super big.
Yenmor hmor tyere tyere. The car goes super quickly.

For words that are longer than two syllables, speakers usually stick to the suffix "-os" for simplicity and ease of pronunciation.
