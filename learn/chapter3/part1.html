Part 1: Tenses

Simple Tenses

Pwekish features five basic tenses: present, past, future, continuous, and habitual. Let's start with the most straightforward ones.

Simple Present Tense

Every verb is in the present tense by default. To use it, simply use the base form of the verb without modification. For example, the sentence "I eat" translates to "Ņra dip". "Dip" is the base form.

Simple Past Tense & Present Perfect Tense

To put a verb into the past tense, add the prefix "hwi-" /hʷi/ . For instance, "hwidip" means "ate". In Pwekish, there is no grammatical distinction between the simple past tense and the present perfect tense. Therefore, the sentences "I ate an apple." and "I have eaten an apple." both translate to "Ņra bimbo hwidip.".

Simple Future Tense

To put a verb into the future tense, add the prefix "nis-" /nis/ . For instance, "nisdip" means "will eat".

PVIM and Tenses

Before moving on to the remaining tenses, there is a crucial grammatical rule to keep in mind:

Word Order Rule: You must apply PVIM to the verb after adding the tense prefix. The mutation affects the newly formed beginning of the word.

For example, if you want to say "it ate" (third person + past tense + eat) in Pwekish:

  1. Take the root verb: dip
  2. Add the past tense prefix: hwidip
  3. Apply 3rd-person PVIM to the initial letter "h": nhwidip

Note: Conjugating the root instead (e.g., "hwidhip") is grammatically incorrect.

Simple Tenses (Continued)

Now that we have covered present, past, and future, let's explore the remaining two basic tenses: continuous and habitual.

Continuous Tense

Pwekish features three types of continuous tenses: present continuous, past continuous, and future continuous. Just like before, we add specific prefixes to the verbs.

Present Continuous: "kut-" /kut/

Past Continuous: "hwikut-" /ˈhʷikut/

Future Continuous: "niskut-" /ˈniskut/

Example: "I am running." -> "Ņra kutteptep.".

Habitual Tense

The habitual tense is used to describe actions that are done regularly or as a habit ("always" or "used to"). It also spans three timeframes.

Present Habitual: "mol-" /mol/

Past Habitual: "hwimol-" /ˈhʷimol/

Future Habitual: "nismol-" /ˈnismol/

Examples: "He always drinks." -> "Nrow hmoldipxek.". "I used to eat fish." -> "Ņra sewu hwimoldip.".

Volitional Form

The volitional form is used to make suggestions that include both the listener and the speaker, equivalent to "let's" in English. While it is technically a mood rather than a tense, it functions using prefixes in the same way.

To make a verb volitional, add the prefix "byu-" /bʲu/ .

Example: "byumor" -> "let's go".

Grammar Note: The volitional form is always used in the present tense and never undergoes PVIM.
